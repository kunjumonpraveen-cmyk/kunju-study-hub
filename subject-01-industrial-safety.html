What this subject is about

Industrial safety is not simply “following rules.” It is a systematic approach to identifying hazards, evaluating risk, selecting effective controls, learning from incidents and maintaining safe work conditions.

Core idea: A good safety professional does not wait for an accident to prove that a hazard exists. The objective is to identify credible unwanted events early and control them before harm occurs.

The safety chain

A useful mental model for this course is:

HazardSomething with the potential to cause harm.
ExposurePeople, assets or environment coming into contact with the hazard.
RiskCombination of likelihood and consequence.
ControlA measure intended to eliminate or reduce the risk.
IncidentAn undesired event; outcomes may range from none to serious harm.
LearningUsing evidence to prevent recurrence and improve the system.

Unit-wise study material

Open each unit to study the core syllabus. These sections are designed to become the foundation for deeper lessons, diagrams and case studies later.

Unit I — Safety fundamentals & terminology
  • Meaning, objectives and scope of occupational and industrial safety.
  • Safety, health, environment, security and emergency preparedness — how they differ and interact.
  • Basic terms: hazard, risk, exposure, incident, accident, near miss, unsafe act, unsafe condition and control measure.
  • Why prevention is preferable to relying on response after an event.
  • Safety responsibilities of management, supervisors, workers, contractors and safety professionals.
  • Safety policy, objectives, accountability and the importance of visible leadership.
  • Introduction to safety management systems and continual improvement.
Unit II — Accident causation & incident learning
  • Why accidents are usually the result of multiple contributing factors rather than one isolated mistake.
  • Unsafe acts and unsafe conditions as immediate contributors.
  • Human factors, supervision, procedures, training, equipment condition and organisational factors.
  • Basic accident-causation models: sequential models, multiple-causation thinking and barrier concepts.
  • Incident, accident and near-miss reporting.
  • Immediate, underlying and root/system causes — and why “operator error” is often an incomplete conclusion.
  • Corrective and preventive actions: making actions specific, responsible, measurable and verifiable.
Unit III — Hazard identification & risk assessment
  • Hazard categories: physical, chemical, biological, ergonomic, mechanical, electrical and process-related hazards.
  • Hazard identification through observation, task analysis, inspection, incident history and worker consultation.
  • Risk as a combination of likelihood and consequence.
  • Risk matrices and their limitations — a matrix is a decision aid, not a substitute for engineering judgement.
  • Hierarchy of controls: elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls and PPE.
  • Residual risk and the need to verify that controls actually work.
Professional habit: When recommending a control, ask “Can the hazard be removed or engineered out?” before jumping directly to PPE or instructions.
Unit IV — Workplace inspections & safety observations
  • Purpose and types of inspections: routine, planned, focused, statutory/regulatory where applicable, and post-incident.
  • Inspection planning: scope, checklist, responsible persons, area boundaries and evidence to collect.
  • Observing housekeeping, access/egress, guarding, electrical systems, lifting, work at height, PPE, emergency equipment and chemical storage.
  • Recording findings using objective language: what was observed, where, when and what evidence supports the finding.
  • Prioritising findings according to risk and assigning corrective actions.
  • Closing the loop: verification of corrective action rather than merely marking an item “closed.”
Unit V — Safety culture, communication & management systems
  • Safety culture and the influence of leadership, supervision, reporting climate and workforce involvement.
  • Toolbox meetings and pre-job safety communication.
  • Safety committees, worker participation and consultation.
  • Training, competency and communication of critical controls.
  • Safety performance indicators: leading and lagging indicators.
  • Safety audits, management review and continual improvement.
  • Professional ethics: accurate reporting, evidence-based recommendations and refusing to hide unsafe conditions.

Key concepts to remember

Hazard ≠ RiskA hazard is a source of potential harm; risk considers the possibility and severity of that harm.
Near miss mattersA near miss can reveal failed controls before a serious outcome occurs.
Hierarchy mattersHigher-order controls are generally more reliable than relying only on behaviour or PPE.
Inspection ≠ AuditAn inspection looks for conditions and behaviours; an audit evaluates a management system against defined criteria.
Cause ≠ BlameInvestigation should identify contributing conditions and system weaknesses, not simply assign fault.
Closure needs evidenceA corrective action is not effectively closed until implementation and effectiveness are verified.

Practical & skill exercises

The practical component should force students to observe, analyse and communicate — not merely reproduce definitions.

01 · Workplace safety inspection

Inspect a selected workplace area and record at least 10 observations with evidence and recommended controls.

02 · Hazard identification

Select one routine task, identify hazards, affected persons, possible consequences and existing controls.

03 · Toolbox talk

Prepare a 5–10 minute toolbox talk on a high-risk task using a clear introduction, hazards, controls and worker questions.

04 · Near-miss report

Write a factual near-miss report without blame language. Separate what happened from why it happened.

05 · Risk assessment

Use a simple likelihood × consequence approach, then show how the risk changes after controls are introduced.

06 · Corrective-action tracking

Assign an action owner, target date, evidence requirement and verification step for inspection findings.
