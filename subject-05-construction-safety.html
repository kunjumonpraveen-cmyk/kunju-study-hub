1. Construction Safety Fundamentals

Construction sites are dynamic workplaces. Conditions, workers, equipment and hazards can change rapidly. A safe system therefore needs planning, competent supervision, coordination and continual inspection.

Common construction hazards

Falls

Edges, openings, roofs, scaffolds, ladders and fragile surfaces.

Struck-by

Vehicles, falling objects, moving equipment and suspended loads.

Caught-in

Machinery, trenches, rotating equipment and collapsing structures.

Electrical

Temporary supplies, damaged cables, overhead lines and energised systems.

Excavation

Collapse, underground services, water ingress and hazardous atmospheres.

Fire

Hot work, fuel, temporary electrics, LPG and combustible materials.

Core principle: A construction safety system must be designed for change. Yesterday's safe inspection does not prove today's condition is safe.

2. Work at Height

Work at height exists whenever a person could fall far enough to suffer harm. The exact legal definition varies by jurisdiction, but the safety principle is universal: prevent the fall where reasonably practicable.

Hierarchy for fall prevention

  1. Avoid the height task where possible.
  2. Prevent the fall using a safe working platform, guardrails or equivalent collective protection.
  3. Minimise consequences using appropriate fall-restraint or fall-arrest systems when prevention is not reasonably practicable.

Inspection points

CheckLook for
Edge protectionSecure guardrails, toe boards, suitable openings protection.
Working platformStable, adequately supported, suitable for the task and free from dangerous gaps.
AccessSafe access and egress; no improvised climbing.
WeatherWind, rain, ice or other conditions affecting stability and footing.
Falling objectsTool control, exclusion zones and material securing.
Fall-arrest is not automatically safe. A fall-arrest system requires suitable equipment, anchorage, clearance, rescue planning and competent use. Suspension after a fall can itself create serious risk.

3. Scaffolding

Scaffolds are temporary structures that must be properly designed or selected, erected, altered, inspected and used for their intended purpose.

Basic scaffold inspection

Foundation

Stable base and suitable bearing; no unsafe settlement.

Structure

Sound components, appropriate bracing and stability.

Platform

Complete, secure and suitable for intended loading.

Edge protection

Guardrails, toe boards and protected openings as required.

Access

Safe stairs, ladders or other designed access.

Inspection status

Required inspections completed and deficiencies addressed.

Never modify a scaffold casually. Removing a brace, guardrail or component can change the structural system and must follow the approved method and competent control.

4. Ladders

Ladders are primarily access equipment and, depending on the task and rules, may be unsuitable for prolonged or demanding work.

Pre-use checks

  • Correct type and length.
  • Sound stiles, rungs, feet and locking devices.
  • Clean, dry and free of contamination.
  • Stable footing and suitable positioning.
  • Safe clearance from electrical hazards.
  • Secure against movement where necessary.
Do not use a damaged ladder. Tag it out and remove it from service according to site procedure.

5. Excavation & Trenching

Excavations can collapse suddenly and can also contain underground services, water, contaminated soil or hazardous atmospheres.

Main hazards

Collapse

Unstable sides can bury or crush workers.

Services

Gas, electricity, water and communications may be underground.

Falling loads

Plant, spoil and materials can enter the excavation.

Water

Flooding can destabilise sides and create drowning hazards.

Atmosphere

Low oxygen or toxic/flammable gases may occur.

Access/egress

Workers need safe ways in and out.

Controls

Use competent assessment and an appropriate protective system such as sloping, benching, shoring or other engineered methods as required by the site and applicable standards. Keep spoil and plant away from edges where they could contribute to collapse.

6. Electrical Safety

Electrical injuries can result from shock, burns, arc flash, arc blast, fires and explosions. Construction environments add risk because systems are temporary and exposed to mechanical damage and changing conditions.

Basic principles

De-energise

Where practicable, isolate the circuit before work.

Lockout/tagout

Prevent unexpected energisation and clearly identify isolation.

Verify

Test for absence of voltage using appropriate procedures and equipment.

Protect

Use suitable enclosures, grounding/earthing and protective devices.

Inspect

Check cables, plugs, tools and temporary distribution equipment.

Competence

Only authorised and competent persons should perform electrical work.

Never assume a circuit is dead because a switch is off. Isolation must be established and verified using the approved procedure.

7. Lifting Operations

Lifting incidents can involve catastrophic consequences because loads may be heavy, elevated and dynamic.

Are accessories suitable?Slings, shackles and hooks must be compatible and rated.

Key terms

WLL / SWL must never be exceeded

Use the terminology and capacity markings specified by the equipment manufacturer and applicable standards. Inspect lifting accessories before use and remove defective items from service.

Never stand or work under a suspended load. Establish an effective exclusion zone and control access.

8. Machinery Guarding & Mechanical Safety

Machines can expose workers to rotating parts, nip points, cutting tools, crushing zones, stored energy and unexpected movement.

Common dangerous points

Nip point

Where rotating or moving parts can draw in a person or body part.

Shear point

Moving components can cut or shear material.

Crush point

Two surfaces can close together and trap a person.

Guarding principles

  • Prevent access to dangerous moving parts.
  • Prevent guards from being easily defeated.
  • Interlock guards where appropriate.
  • Provide emergency stopping arrangements where required.
  • Isolate energy before maintenance.

9. Hot Work: Welding, Cutting & Grinding

Hot work introduces ignition sources into environments that may contain combustible materials, flammable liquids, gases or vapours.

Typical controls

Permit to work

Formal authorisation where required by the site system.

Gas testing

Test for flammable or toxic atmospheres where relevant.

Remove combustibles

Clear or protect combustible materials and openings.

Fire watch

Provide competent fire-watch arrangements where required.

Extinguishing equipment

Keep appropriate firefighting equipment immediately available.

Post-work monitoring

Check for smouldering or heat transfer after work.

Hot work can start a fire hours after the work appears finished. Post-work inspection and monitoring must match the risk and site procedure.

10. Confined Spaces

A confined space is not simply “a small room.” The applicable definition depends on jurisdiction and the characteristics of the space. The key concern is that entry can expose workers to serious hazards and rescue can be difficult.

Potential hazards

Oxygen deficiency

Insufficient oxygen for safe work.

Toxic atmosphere

Hazardous gases or vapours.

Flammable atmosphere

Potential for fire or explosion.

Engulfment

Liquids, solids or materials can bury or overwhelm a worker.

Mechanical energy

Agitators, valves or equipment may move unexpectedly.

Rescue

Entry may be difficult and spontaneous rescue can create multiple casualties.

Entry system

Where required, use a documented entry procedure covering hazard assessment, isolation, atmospheric testing, ventilation, communication, attendant arrangements, PPE, permit controls and a realistic rescue plan.

Never enter a suspected hazardous atmosphere to perform an improvised rescue. Follow the trained rescue system.

11. Housekeeping & Material Storage

Good housekeeping is a control measure, not merely an appearance standard.

Walkways

Keep routes clear and eliminate trip hazards.

Storage

Stack materials securely and within safe load limits.

Waste

Remove combustible and hazardous waste promptly.

Spills

Control and clean spills using appropriate procedures.

12. Temporary Electrical & Site Services

Temporary systems can be exposed to weather, traffic, mechanical damage and frequent modification.

Inspection points

  • Distribution boards protected from damage and unauthorised access.
  • Cables routed to prevent crushing, cutting and trip hazards.
  • Suitable protection against electric shock and overcurrent.
  • Connections protected from water and environmental exposure.
  • Temporary modifications controlled and documented.
  • Portable tools and equipment inspected according to site requirements.

Essential Glossary

Fall arrest

System intended to stop a fall after it occurs and limit consequences.

Fall restraint

System that prevents a worker from reaching a fall position.

Guardrail

Collective edge protection intended to prevent falls.

Shoring

Structural support used to prevent excavation collapse.

WLL

Working Load Limit specified for lifting equipment or accessories.

LOTO

Lockout/tagout used to control hazardous energy.

Hot work

Work capable of producing flame, sparks or sufficient heat to ignite combustibles.

Confined space

Defined by applicable requirements; generally a space with restricted entry/egress and significant hazards.

Competent person

A person with the knowledge, training and experience required to perform a defined safety function.
