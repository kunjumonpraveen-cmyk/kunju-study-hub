1. What Is Process Safety?

Process safety is a systematic framework for preventing and mitigating major accidents arising from loss of containment, hazardous process conditions and failures of safety-critical systems.

Occupational safety

Often focuses on events such as slips, falls, struck-by injuries and routine task hazards.

Process safety

Focuses on catastrophic scenarios such as explosions, toxic releases, major fires and uncontrolled reactions.

Process Safety = Prevent major releases + Prevent escalation + Mitigate consequences
Important: A facility can have an excellent personal-safety record and still have serious process-safety weaknesses. Low injury frequency does not prove major-accident risk is controlled.

2. Major Accident Hazards

A major accident involves a serious uncontrolled event with potential for multiple fatalities, major environmental damage or extensive asset damage.

Typical scenarios

Loss of containment

Pipeline, vessel, tank, flange or seal failure.

Overpressure

Blocked outlet, runaway reaction, fire exposure or control failure.

Runaway reaction

Reaction rate or heat generation exceeds control capability.

Fire

Pool, jet or flash fire affecting people and equipment.

Explosion

Combustion of a flammable mixture with potentially damaging pressure effects.

Toxic release

Release of hazardous substances such as toxic gases or vapours.

3. HAZID — Hazard Identification

HAZID is a structured workshop used to identify hazards and credible accident scenarios early in a project or for an existing operation.

Typical questions

  • What hazardous materials are present?
  • What energies are present?
  • What could fail?
  • What could initiate the failure?
  • Who or what could be affected?
  • What safeguards already exist?
  • What additional controls are required?
Good HAZID output: clearly defined hazards, credible scenarios, consequences, existing safeguards, actions and responsible owners.

4. HAZOP

Hazard and Operability Study (HAZOP) is a structured team-based examination of process design intent using guide words to identify deviations, causes, consequences, safeguards and recommendations.

Core structure

ElementQuestion
Design intentWhat should normally happen?
ParameterFlow, pressure, temperature, level, composition, etc.
Guide wordNo, More, Less, Reverse, Other Than, etc.
DeviationWhat abnormal condition results?
CauseWhy could it happen?
ConsequenceWhat could happen next?
SafeguardWhat prevents or mitigates it?
RecommendationWhat improvement is justified?
Guide word + parameter → deviation → cause → consequence → safeguard

5. Process Risk Assessment

Risk assessment should be scenario-based. Identify the initiating event, consequence, existing safeguards and residual risk.

Risk concepts

Frequency

How often could the initiating event or scenario occur?

Consequence

What is the credible severity if it occurs?

Residual risk

Risk remaining after existing controls are considered.

Risk matrices can be useful communication tools, but they should not replace engineering judgement, scenario analysis or appropriate quantitative methods where required.

6. Bow-Tie Analysis

Bow-Tie analysis places the top event in the centre, with threats on the left and consequences on the right. Barriers are shown between threats and the top event, and between the top event and consequences.

Threats → Preventive Barriers → TOP EVENT → Mitigative Barriers → Consequences

Example

SideExample
ThreatHigh pressure, corrosion, valve failure.
Preventive barrierPressure control, inspection, high-pressure trip.
Top eventLoss of containment.
Mitigative barrierGas detection, ESD, firewater, emergency response.
ConsequenceFire, explosion, toxic exposure or environmental release.

7. Layers of Protection

A major hazard should not depend on a single fragile safeguard. Multiple layers can reduce the likelihood or consequence of an accident.

Inherent design

Reduce hazardous inventory, pressure or energy where feasible.

Basic process control

Normal control systems keep the process within its operating envelope.

Alarm & operator action

Warn the operator and provide defined response.

SIS / trip

Independent automated protective action for specified scenarios.

Physical protection

Relief systems, containment or passive protection where appropriate.

Emergency response

Fire protection, evacuation, isolation and emergency organisation.

Barrier independence matters. If one common failure can defeat several supposed safeguards simultaneously, they may not provide the independence assumed in the risk assessment.

8. Management of Change (MOC)

Changes that appear small can create major hazards when they alter process conditions, equipment, materials, procedures or protection layers.

Changes that may require review

Technical

Equipment, piping, control logic, set points or materials.

Process

Pressure, temperature, flow, chemistry or operating envelope.

Organisational

Roles, staffing or responsibilities affecting safety-critical work.

Temporary

Temporary hoses, bypasses, scaffolds, equipment or operating changes.

MOC sequence

  1. Define the proposed change.
  2. Identify hazards introduced or altered.
  3. Review safeguards and affected procedures.
  4. Obtain required technical approval.
  5. Implement the change under controlled conditions.
  6. Update drawings, procedures and training.
  7. Verify completion before returning to normal operation.

9. Mechanical Integrity

Mechanical integrity means ensuring safety-critical equipment remains fit for its intended service throughout its lifecycle.

EquipmentIntegrity concern
Pressure vesselsCorrosion, cracking, overpressure and loss of containment.
PipingWall thinning, corrosion, erosion, vibration and mechanical damage.
Relief devicesCorrect set pressure, capacity, condition and discharge arrangement.
Rotating equipmentSeals, bearings, vibration and overheating.
Safety systemsAvailability, proof testing, faults and bypass management.
Inspection is not the same as integrity. Integrity requires a defined strategy, acceptance criteria, inspection/testing intervals, defect evaluation and timely corrective action.

10. Safety Instrumented Systems

A Safety Instrumented System (SIS) detects defined hazardous conditions and takes a predetermined action to move the process toward a safer state.

Basic architecture

Sensor → Logic Solver → Final Element
Sensor

Detects the process condition.

Logic solver

Determines whether the trip condition is met.

Final element

Performs the protective action, such as closing a valve.

Safety instrumented functions should have defined requirements, appropriate independence, testing and management of bypasses and failures.

11. Operating Procedures & Safe Operating Limits

Procedures translate process design and hazard knowledge into repeatable operating actions.

Procedures should address

  • Normal startup and shutdown.
  • Normal operating limits.
  • Abnormal and emergency conditions.
  • Alarm response.
  • Safe operating envelope.
  • Temporary operations and deviations.
  • Isolation and permit requirements.
Alarm flooding is a process-safety problem. An alarm system should help operators recognise and respond to meaningful abnormal conditions rather than overwhelm them with nuisance alarms.

12. Competence & Training

Process safety depends on people understanding the hazards, the process limits, the protective systems and their own authority to stop unsafe work.

Initial competence

Verify knowledge and practical ability before assigning safety-critical duties.

Refresher training

Maintain competence and address changes or identified weaknesses.

Emergency drills

Exercise realistic response roles and identify organisational weaknesses.

Learning from events

Use incidents and near misses to improve competence and systems.

13. Contractor Process Safety

Contractors can introduce additional risk because they may be unfamiliar with the facility, hazards, permit systems and emergency arrangements.

Controls

  • Prequalification based on capability and risk.
  • Site-specific induction.
  • Clear scope and responsibilities.
  • Permit-to-work compliance.
  • Competency verification.
  • Supervision proportional to risk.
  • Performance monitoring and learning.

14. Incident Investigation

A process-safety investigation should seek the underlying technical, organisational and management-system causes rather than stopping at “operator error.”

Useful questions

  1. What happened?
  2. What was the actual sequence?
  3. Which barriers failed or were absent?
  4. Why did those barriers fail?
  5. Were procedures, design assumptions or maintenance practices inadequate?
  6. What systemic changes will prevent recurrence?
Good investigation outcome: actions that address failed systems and barriers, with owners, deadlines and effectiveness verification.

15. Emergency Planning

Major-accident emergency plans should be based on credible scenarios rather than generic instructions.

Detection

Alarm and communication systems.

People

Evacuation, muster and accountability.

Isolation

Emergency shutdown and source control.

Fire protection

Firewater, foam, monitors and trained response.

Medical

First aid, casualty management and external support.

Escalation

Domino prevention and decision criteria.

16. Auditing & Process-Safety Indicators

Leading indicators examine whether important preventive controls are functioning; lagging indicators examine events that have already occurred.

TypeExample
LeadingOverdue safety-critical inspections, proof-test completion, relief-device backlog.
LeadingOpen critical MOC actions or overdue barrier tests.
LaggingLoss-of-containment events or major fires.
LaggingUncontrolled releases exceeding defined thresholds.
Strong organisations monitor weak signals. Waiting for a catastrophic event before measuring process-safety performance is poor practice.

Essential Glossary

Process safety

Framework for preventing and mitigating major accidents from hazardous process conditions.

HAZID

Structured hazard-identification study.

HAZOP

Structured examination of process deviations using guide words.

Bow-Tie

Barrier-analysis method connecting threats, top event and consequences.

Barrier

Measure intended to prevent an event or mitigate its consequences.

MOC

Management of Change.

Mechanical integrity

Ensuring safety-critical equipment remains fit for service.

SIS

Safety Instrumented System.

SIF

Safety Instrumented Function.

LOPA

Layer of Protection Analysis, a structured method for evaluating independent protection layers.

Top event

Loss of control of a hazard, often loss of containment in a Bow-Tie.

Safety-critical equipment

Equipment whose failure can contribute to or fail to prevent a major accident.
